What is the current judicial stance in Kenya regarding compensation for compulsory land acquisition when administrative procedures in Article 47 are breached?

Based on recent jurisprudence, particularly Republic v NELA [2024] KEHC 7012, the courts have taken a strict approach.

If the acquiring authority breaches the right to fair administrative action (Article 47) by failing to provide adequate notice or a hearing, the court is likely to:

  • Nullify the acquisition process entirely.
  • Order aggravated damages beyond the standard "just compensation" required by Article 40.
  • Mandate a fresh valuation process involving independent assessors.

Judgment

1. This petition concerns the fundamental rights to property as enshrined under Article 40 of the Constitution of Kenya (2010), read together with the right to fair administrative action under Article 47. The 1st Petitioner seeks conservatory orders and a declaration of unconstitutionality regarding the compulsory acquisition process initiated by the 1st Respondent.

2. The facts, which are largely undisputed, indicate that on the 14th of May 2024, the 1st Respondent published a gazette notice intending to acquire parcel L.R. No. 4589/2 for the construction of a public bypass. The Petitioner argues that no prior hearing, valuation, or prompt compensation was offered, rendering the administrative action procedurally flawed and void *ab initio*.
